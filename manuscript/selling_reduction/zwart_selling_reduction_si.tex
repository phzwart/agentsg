\emph{Specialisations.} The conorms of any member, in any labelling, are integer combinations of the reduced cell's conorms, hence linear in the free parameters of the sort (the letters of Table~4 of the main text); imposing another sort's zeros and equalities on one member is a homogeneous linear system in those parameters, and a solution with non-negative parameters, a non-singular lattice and a resulting lattice of the target sort and no higher is a specialisation. Table~\ref{tab:special} lists the conditions of codimension one, on 36 pairs of sorts.\chk{z50} Each is a parameter vanishing or two parameters becoming equal. The classical values appear: body-centred tetragonal is face-centred cubic at $c/a=\sqrt2$ and body-centred cubic at $c/a=1$; C-centred orthorhombic is hexagonal at $b=\sqrt3\,a$ and tetragonal at $b=a$; rhombohedral is cubic at $\alpha=60^\circ$, $90^\circ$ and $109.47^\circ$. The monoclinic conditions come in threes, one per shape: with unique axis $c$ and plane lattice $a,b$, the lattice is C-centred orthorhombic when $|a|=|b|$, $|a|=|a+b|$ or $|b|=|a+b|$, and primitive orthorhombic when $a\perp b$, $a\perp(a+b)$ or $b\perp(a+b)$ --- the textbook condition $a=c$ read in each of the three cell choices. The table also shows which sorts are one condition from a much higher symmetry: C-centred monoclinic from tetragonal, body-centred tetragonal and rhombohedral; the body-centred orthorhombic boundary sort from face-centred cubic; triclinic on a wall from C-centred monoclinic.

\section{Walks on the measured metric, and the benchmark}
\label{si:bench}
The repository directory \texttt{manuscript/benchmark} holds 780 measured cells with exact references and true operators, in five families: 75 cells measured in a different shape of the same lattice; 660 lattices placed $0.5$ to $8$ times the error from a condition of Table~\ref{tab:special}; 20 cells whose only reduced superbase is a difference-wall member; 16 needles and plates with angular errors; 9 cells of the boundary sorts. Each case also carries its Niggli cell. \texttt{evaluate.py} scores the conorm-band methods of Appendix~B of the main text and \texttt{evaluate\_strain.py} the strain-norm procedure, by whether the true operator is proposed from one reduction of the measured cell; the results are in \texttt{results.txt} and \texttt{results\_strain.txt}.

\section{The operators alone}
\label{app:operators}
%==============================================================================

The closure can be described without any lattice. An operator $\Wop$, written as the new basis in the coordinates of the reduced cell, acts on the six conorms by a fixed integer $6\times6$ matrix $\mathbf A_{\Wop}$, since every conorm of $\Wop F_0$ is an integer combination of the conorms of $F_0$. The reduced cells are the points of the orthant $\{p\ge0\}$ in conorm space; Selling (1874) showed that every lattice has a conorm vector in this orthant, and it is unique up to the 48 swaps except on the faces (Delaunay, 1933; Conway \& Sloane, 1992), and the closure of a lattice is the set of points of its orbit that lie in the orthant: one in the interior, several on the faces. So $\Wop$ is an operator of the closure of every lattice with zero pattern $Z$ exactly when $\mathbf A_{\Wop}$ maps the face $\{p\ge0,\ p_Z=0\}$ into the orthant, which is a sign condition on the entries of $\mathbf A_{\Wop}$ and nothing else. Enumerating integer matrices under that condition gives the operator set of each face without a lattice: $48\times1$, $2$, $3$, $6$ and $16$ matrices for no zero, one zero, two zeros on disjoint edges, two zeros on a path and three zeros, and their union, over all placements of the faces, is a set $\mathcal U$ of $6096=48\times127$ integer matrices with entries in $\{-3,\dots,3\}$. $\mathcal U$ is closed under inversion, every element has a labelling whose basis vectors have entries in $\{-2,\dots,2\}$, and $\mathcal U$ contains every operator between two reduced cells of one lattice, whatever the lattice: an operator out of any member is an operator out of a reduced cell of that member's zero pattern, Lemma~A1 of the main text confines the members of a closure to superbase coordinates of modulus at most 7 relative to that cell, and the exhaustive enumeration of that window finds only members with coordinates in $\{-1,0,1\}$, so every such operator has entries in $\{-1,0,1\}$ in the labelling of its source and lies within the window enumerated.\chk{z52,z56} The three-zero face enumerated is the triangle; its path placement gives the same operators relabelled. Up to relabelling at both ends $\mathcal U$ has nine kinds of element, of flip length $0$ to $4$ (Table~\ref{tab:kinds}).

\emph{Why four.} For a lattice with $a\perp b\perp c$ the sixteen members are four orthogonal superbases, differing by the sign of one axis, and twelve with a face diagonal. A flip from an orthogonal cell always gives a cell with a face diagonal, so changing the sign of one axis takes three flips, out, across and in, and the diagonal cells attached only to the far orthogonal cell take one more: from $(a,b,c)$ the members lie at flip distances $0,1,2,3,4$ in numbers $1,3,6,3,3$, and four is the diameter of the largest face.\chk{z46}
