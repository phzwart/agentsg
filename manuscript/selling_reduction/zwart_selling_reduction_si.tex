\emph{Specialisations.} The conorms of any member, in any labelling, are integer combinations of the reduced cell's conorms, hence linear in the free parameters of the sort (the letters of Table~\ref{tab:sorts}). Imposing another sort's zeros and equalities on one member is a homogeneous linear system in those parameters, and a solution with non-negative parameters, a non-singular lattice and a resulting lattice of the target sort and no higher is a specialisation. Table~\ref{tab:special} lists the conditions of codimension one, on 36 pairs of sorts. Each is a parameter vanishing or two parameters becoming equal. The classical values appear: body-centred tetragonal is face-centred cubic at $c/a=\sqrt2$ and body-centred cubic at $c/a=1$; C-centred orthorhombic is hexagonal at $b=\sqrt3\,a$ and tetragonal at $b=a$; rhombohedral is cubic at $\alpha=60^\circ$, $90^\circ$ and $109.47^\circ$. The monoclinic conditions come in threes, one per shape: with unique axis $c$ and plane lattice $a,b$, the lattice is C-centred orthorhombic when $|a|=|b|$, $|a|=|a+b|$ or $|b|=|a+b|$, and primitive orthorhombic when $a\perp b$, $a\perp(a+b)$ or $b\perp(a+b)$ --- the textbook condition $a=c$ read in each of the three cell choices. The table also shows which sorts are one condition from a much higher symmetry: C-centred monoclinic from tetragonal, body-centred tetragonal and rhombohedral; the body-centred orthorhombic boundary sort from face-centred cubic; triclinic on a wall from C-centred monoclinic.

\section{The benchmark, the comparisons and the walks}
\label{si:bench}
%==============================================================================

\emph{The benchmark.} The directory \texttt{manuscript/benchmark} of the repository holds 780 measured cells with exact references and true operators, in five families: 75 cells measured in a different shape of the same lattice; 660 lattices placed $0.5$ to $8$ times the error from a condition of Table~\ref{tab:special}; 20 cells whose only reduced superbase is a difference-wall member; 16 needles and plates with angular errors; 9 cells of the boundary sorts. Each case carries its Niggli cell. The errors are isotropic in the metric tensor, of median strain $0.1\%$. A case counts as found when the true proper operator itself is among the candidates. \texttt{evaluate\_strain.py} scores the closure and the $\{-1,0,1\}$ search at the band and at three times each case's error (\texttt{results\_strain.txt}); \texttt{evaluate.py --niggli} tallies the Niggli cells by family and sort (\texttt{results\_niggli.txt}). The measured Niggli cell is a different cell from the reference's in 45 cases (O1B 23, O3 12, M1B 6, one each at M2B, M3, R3 and T2; 14 of them a change between type~I and type~II), and the same cell in another basis in 125 (O1B 25, M1A 15, O1A 15, T1 15, M1B 13, O4 9, O2 7, R1 7, M2A 6, T2 6, O3 5, A2 1, A3 1).

\emph{The comparisons.} \texttt{compare\_methods.py} runs the methods of Table~9 of the main text on the benchmark (\texttt{results\_compare.txt}) and, with \texttt{--stress}, on the stress family (\texttt{results\_stress.txt}): 60 references drawn from the benchmark, each with independent traceless errors on the reference and the new cell, scaled so that the true operator's price is $t$ times the band, $t=0.5$, $0.9$, $1.1$ and $1.5$, and a random proper reindexing with entries in $\{-2,\dots,2\}$. Each method's candidates are made proper, priced by the geodesic distance after the best scale, and kept within the band; at $t=1.1$ and $1.5$ no method found the true operator, as by construction none should. \texttt{window\_failures.py} builds the window family (\texttt{results\_window\_failures.txt}): 300 lattices of the sorts M4, O4, H4, O5, T5 and C5 with random parameters, each measured twice with an error of $0.1$ to $2\%$, the new cell in a random proper basis and the reference given as its Niggli cell, classified by the Niggli type of the reference and by the kind of face (with an exact reference in the labelling of the sort's pattern the zeros never sit on $d$ and no window fails); the same 300 lattices, drawn again, with the reference in a random proper basis; and 60 lattices on random two- and three-zero faces whose closure has a member needing an entry of $3$, measured in that member. \texttt{coset\_rank.py} groups the closure's candidates by the exact symmetries of the underlying lattice (\texttt{results\_coset.txt}). \texttt{niggli\_types.py} reduces near-monoclinic cells from their Niggli cells and records where the near-zero conorms land (\texttt{results\_niggli\_types.txt}). \texttt{face\_labellings.py} gives, for every face, the largest entry of the operators out of the reduced cell as reduction returns it and in its best labelling (\texttt{results\_face\_labellings.txt}). \texttt{lookup\_benchmark.py} runs the lookup of $\mathcal U$ on the benchmark and the stress family and counts the references whose $r_1$ does not certify it (\texttt{results\_lookup.txt}). \texttt{adjacency\_radii.py} computes $r_1$ for the benchmark references and for 2000 PDB cells drawn at random, and times the widening and the reindexing (\texttt{results\_adjacency\_radii.txt}).

\section{The operators alone}
\label{app:operators}
%==============================================================================

The closure can be described without any lattice. An operator $\Wop$, written as the new basis in the coordinates of the reduced cell, acts on the six conorms by a fixed integer $6\times6$ matrix $\mathbf A_{\Wop}$, since every conorm of $\Wop F_0$ is an integer combination of the conorms of $F_0$. The reduced cells are the points of the orthant $\{p\ge0\}$ in conorm space. Selling (1874) showed that every lattice has a conorm vector in this orthant, unique up to the 48 swaps except on the faces (Delaunay, 1933; Conway \& Sloane, 1992), and the closure of a lattice is the set of points of its orbit that lie in the orthant: one in the interior, several on the faces. So $\Wop$ is an operator of the closure of every lattice on the face of $Z$ exactly when $\mathbf A_{\Wop}$ maps the face $\{p\ge0,\ p_Z=0\}$ into the orthant, a sign condition on the entries of $\mathbf A_{\Wop}$ and nothing else.

Enumerating integer matrices with entries in $[-2,2]$ under that condition, over all 38 faces, and closing the result under relabelling at both ends, gives a set $\mathcal U$ of $6096=48\times127$ integer matrices with entries in $\{-3,\dots,3\}$; per face there are $48\times1$, $2$, $3$, $6$ and $16$ matrices for no zero, one zero, two zeros on disjoint pairs, two zeros sharing a vector and three zeros. Both placements of three zeros, the triangle and the path, were enumerated; they describe the same lattices read from an orthogonal cell or from a cell with a face diagonal, each has 16 members, and no swap carries one placement into the other. $\mathcal U$ is closed under inversion, and every element has a labelling whose basis vectors have entries in $[-2,2]$. That $\mathcal U$ contains every operator between two reduced cells of one lattice, whatever the lattice, does not rest on that window: Lemma~A1 of the main text confines the members of every closure to superbase coordinates $s+2k$ with $|k_i|\le3$ relative to a reduced cell, and the enumeration of that whole window, over all 38 faces, finds only members that lie in $\mathcal U$ in each of their 48 labellings. In the basis of the reduced cell their entries are in $\{-1,0,1\}$ when the zero conorms avoid $d$, and up to $3$ otherwise (Table~6 of the main text). Up to relabelling at both ends $\mathcal U$ has nine kinds of element, of flip length $0$ to $4$ (Table~\ref{tab:kinds}).

\emph{Why four.} For a lattice with $a\perp b\perp c$ the sixteen members are four orthogonal superbases, differing by the sign of one axis, and twelve with a face diagonal. A flip from an orthogonal cell always gives a cell with a face diagonal, so changing the sign of one axis takes three flips, out, across and in, and the diagonal cells attached only to the far orthogonal cell take one more: from $(a,b,c)$ the members lie at flip distances $0,1,2,3,4$ in numbers $1,3,6,3,3$, and four is the diameter of the largest face.

\section{A band on the conorms}
\label{app:tau}
%==============================================================================

The simplest widening of the closure is an absolute band $\tau$ in \Ang$^2$ on the conorms,
\begin{equation}
\Sw(\Gmet)=\bigl\{\,F\;:\;\text{all six conorms of }F\text{ under }\Gmet\text{ are}\ \ge-\tau\,\bigr\},
\label{eq:deftau}
\end{equation}
Selling reduction with its boundary moved from $0$ to $-\tau$. This is an $L^\infty$ band on the conorms, a different norm from the strain of the main text; the two are comparable only for cells whose edges are similar in length. It can be computed by idealising: set every conorm of the reduced superbase $F_0$ that is at most $4\tau$ to zero, enumerate the exact closure of the idealised lattice $\Lambda^*$, and keep the members whose conorms under the measured metric are all at least $-\tau$. The construction is exact when $\lambda_1$, the squared length of the shortest lattice vector, exceeds $32\tau$ (Theorem~S4.5), a ratio independent of the scale of the cell: for an isotropic cell it allows an angular error of about $1.8^\circ$. It fails for anisotropic cells, where the band must cover the conorms between the long vectors: on the 16 needles and plates of the benchmark it declined 8. On lattices of five zero patterns with zero conorms placed at random it was exact at every band from $\lambda_1/32$ to $\lambda_1/6$ in which the idealised lattice existed; on dense two-zero grids idealising at $2\tau$ was already exact, at $\tau$ not. For the worked cell at $\tau=20$\,\Ang$^2$, within the hypothesis, it returns the six members of the ring. A new cell constructed so that its only reduced superbase is a difference-wall member of an orthorhombic corner was matched against two stored sets of the reference: the complete set offers three candidates and their negatives; a set built by idealising only at the band has lost the third, $\pm\mathbf I$, the true operator.

\emph{Bands per slot.} A band that scales with the vectors of each superbase, $p_{ij}(F)\ge-\sigma|u_i||u_j|$ on every slot of $F$ (all pairwise angles at least $90^\circ-\arcsin\sigma$), is the natural angular form of the $L^\infty$ band, but the same construction applied with each slot of $F_0$ idealised against its own band is not exact. On the needle $5.58\times5.21\times131.97$\,\Ang, $\gamma=91.12^\circ$, at $\lambda_1/(\sigma l_al_c)=33$ and $40$, it returns 6 superbases where brute force finds 12: the conorm $ab$ survives idealisation because its own band is small, while the members built on $a+c$ carry the same dot product on a slot whose band is about 25 times larger. Idealising until the zeroed slots are consistent across the members of the closure repairs this, and on 240 random needles, plates, isotropic and monoclinic cells it is never wrong (exact in 124, refused in 116), while the single-pass construction is wrong in 9 (z57).

For a superbase $F$ and two metrics, $\kappa_F(\Gmet,\Gmet')$ is the largest change among $F$'s six conorms.

\emph{Proposition S4.1 (characterisation superbase by superbase).} $\Sw(\Gmet)=\{F:F\in\Cl(\Gmet')\text{ for some }\Gmet'\text{ with }\kappa_F(\Gmet,\Gmet')\le\tau\}$. \emph{Proof.} ($\supseteq$) If $F$ is obtuse for $\Gmet'$ and $\kappa_F\le\tau$, every conorm of $F$ under $\Gmet$ is at least $-\tau$. ($\subseteq$) The metric whose conorms in the frame of $F$ are $\max(p_{ij},0)$ exceeds $\Gmet$ by a positive semidefinite term, so it is positive definite and $F$ is obtuse for it.\hfill$\square$

\emph{Lemma S4.2 (class gap).} Let $\Lambda^*$ be obtained from $F_0$ by setting its conorms $\le\theta$ to zero, be positive definite, and let $m^*$ be the smallest conorm of $F_0$ above $\theta$. A superbase not obtuse for $\Lambda^*$ has $e_c\ge4m^*$ in some class. \emph{Proof.} In the frame of $F_0$, Lemma~A2 of the main text gives $e_c(F)=4\sum p^0_{ij}\gamma_{ij}$, and the excess for $\Lambda^*$ is the same sum over the slots above $\theta$. If those terms vanish in all seven classes, $F$ is obtuse for $\Lambda^*$; otherwise some $\gamma_{ij}\ge1$ multiplies a $p^0_{ij}\ge m^*$.\hfill$\square$

\emph{Lemma S4.3 (small excess in the band).} If $\lambda_1>32\tau$ and every conorm of $F$ is at least $-\tau$, then $e_c(F)\le16\tau$ in every class. \emph{Proof.} Call a slot large if $p_{ij}>8\tau$, and let $N$ be the negative slots. The large slots connect the four vectors, since otherwise a split crossed only by slots with $p_{ij}\le8\tau$ has a partial sum of squared length at most $32\tau<\lambda_1$. Lemma~A2 in the frame of $F$ gives $0\le e_c(F)=4\sum_N|p_{ij}|\gamma_{ij}-4\sum_{p_{ij}>0}p_{ij}\gamma_{ij}$, so $\sum_{\rm large}p\gamma\le\tau\sum_N\gamma$. If a large $\gamma$ were positive, Lemma~S4.4(ii) would give $8\tau\sum_{\rm large}\gamma<\tau\sum_N\gamma\le8\tau\sum_{\rm large}\gamma$; so the large $\gamma$ vanish, and Lemma~S4.4(i) gives $e_c\le4\tau\cdot4$.\hfill$\square$

\emph{Lemma S4.4 (two constants on four points).} Let $L$ be a set of slots connecting the four vectors and $N$ disjoint from $L$. (i) If $\gamma_{ij}=0$ on $L$, then $\sum_N\gamma_{ij}\le4$. (ii) If $\sum_L\gamma_{ij}>0$, then $\sum_N\gamma_{ij}\le8\sum_L\gamma_{ij}$. Both bounds are attained. \emph{Proof.} In (i) $|\Delta k|\le1$ on $L$, so all $|k_i|\le3$, and the maximum is a finite enumeration over the 38 connected slot sets, the sets $N$ and the 14 partial sums. In (ii) the ratio is enumerated for $|k_i|\le15$; beyond, some $|k_i|\ge16$, and since $L$ connects the four vertices with $k_0=0$, $Q=\sum_L\Delta k^2\ge16^2/3>75$. For each pattern $(L,N)$ let $\rho_{L,N}$ be the largest generalised eigenvalue of the quadratic form $\sum_N\Delta k^2$ against $\sum_L\Delta k^2$; the largest over all pairs $(L,N)$ is $3+2\sqrt2$. Then $\sum_N\gamma\le\rho_{L,N}Q+\sqrt{|N|\rho_{L,N}Q}$ and $\sum_L\gamma\ge Q-\sqrt{|L|Q}$, and the ratio of these bounds, $(\rho_{L,N}\sqrt Q+\sqrt{|N|\rho_{L,N}})/(\sqrt Q-\sqrt{|L|})$, decreases in $Q$, so it suffices to check it at $Q=75$ for each pattern with its own $\rho_{L,N}$, where it is below $8$ in every case; with the uniform value $3+2\sqrt2$ it would not be. The lemma is computer-assisted. Equality in (ii): $L=\{01,02,13\}$, $N=\{03,12,23\}$, $s=(0,0,1,1)$.\hfill$\square$

\emph{Theorem S4.5.} Let $F_0$ be obtuse for $\Gmet$, $\lambda_1>32\tau$, and $\Lambda^*$ obtained by setting every conorm of $F_0$ that is at most $4\tau$ to zero. Then $\Lambda^*$ is positive definite and $\Sw(\Gmet)=\{F\in\Cl(\Lambda^*):p_{ij}(F;\Gmet)\ge-\tau\}$. \emph{Proof.} If the slots of $F_0$ above $4\tau$ did not connect the four vectors, some partial sum would have squared length at most $16\tau<\lambda_1$. The inclusion $\supseteq$ is the definition. For $F\in\Sw(\Gmet)$, Lemma~S4.3 gives $e_c(F;\Gmet)\le16\tau$; the excess for $\Lambda^*$ is the same sum with the zeroed slots dropped, so $e_c(F;\Lambda^*)\le16\tau<4m^*$, and Lemma~S4.2 makes $F$ obtuse for $\Lambda^*$.\hfill$\square$ The operators from $F_0$ to the members of $\Cl(\Lambda^*)$ are integer matrices independent of the metric, so one enumeration serves every band up to the one idealised at.

\emph{Lemma S4.6 (nesting).} If $Z'\subseteq Z$ are sets of conorms of $F_0$ set to zero, every member of the closure for $Z'$ is a member of the closure for $Z$. \emph{Proof.} The members for $Z'$ are reached from $F_0$ by words of flips, each at a slot that is zero at that point of the word (Lemma~A3 of the main text); a flip at a slot zero for both lattices acts on both conorm vectors as the same transposition of two slots, so after each flip the superbase is obtuse for both and the next flip is again at a slot zero for both.\hfill$\square$

\emph{Proposition S4.7 (reindexing guarantee in the reference frame).} Let $\Gmet_N'$ be the new cell's metric written in the reference's reduced frame, $\|\Gmet_N'-\Gmet_R\|_F\le\eta<\nu_{\min}$ with $\nu_{\min}$ the smallest eigenvalue of $\Gmet_R$, let $F_N$ be its reduced superbase with basis vectors of summed squared length $V$, and let $\tau\ge\sqrt2\,\eta\,V/(\nu_{\min}-\eta)$ with $\lambda_1(\Gmet_R)>32\tau$. Then $F_N$, with its true integer coefficients in the reference frame, is a member of $\Sw(\Gmet_R)$, and pairing it with the new cell's reduced superbase proposes the true operator. \emph{Proof.} With $U$ the coordinates of $F_N$'s basis vectors in the reference frame and $\Delta=\Gmet_N'-\Gmet_R$, $\sum|u_i|^2\le V/(\nu_{\min}-\eta)$; in $F_N$'s own frame the error has Frobenius norm at most $\sum|u_i|^2\,\eta$, and every conorm shift is at most $\sqrt2$ times that norm. The conorms of $F_N$ under $\Gmet_N'$ are non-negative, so under $\Gmet_R$ they are at least $-\tau$, and Theorem~S4.5 applies.\hfill$\square$ For fifteen references near zero conorms of five zero patterns, each with twelve new cells carrying a random metric error and a random reindexing, the true operator was among the candidates in 180 of 180 cases at the band given by the coverage bound below; the guarantee of Proposition~S4.7 is conservative, a median $13\eta$ against a band near $3\eta$ that was enough.

\emph{From a metric error to a band.} A metric error $\Delta\Gmet$ shifts the conorm of a member with coordinate vectors $u_i,u_j$ by $-u_i^{\mathsf T}\Delta\Gmet\,u_j$, at most $c_{ij}\eta$ with $c_{ij}=\|\sym(u_iu_j^{\mathsf T})\|_F$; every member survives every error of norm $\eta$ exactly when $\tau\ge\tau_{\rm cov}=\max(c_{ij}\eta-p_{ij})$, read off the stored closure. The largest constant on zero conorms is $\sqrt2$ with one zero, $\sqrt5$ with two, and $2\sqrt3$ in six of the sixteen labelled cuboid frames, so $\tau=3\eta$ is not a worst-case guarantee in every frame. These frame-dependent constants are what the strain norm removes.

\emph{Against BLDConograph and SELLA} (Appendix~B of the main text). On the seven sample cells that Oishi-Tomiyasu (2024) distributes with BLDConograph, the highest Bravais type within $7.4\%$ is the program's type in all seven, with the next type at least $14.9\%$ away (Table~\ref{tab:conograph}). SELLA was run on the worked cell, the lysozyme cell, a pseudo-tetragonal cell, a cell with all angles $92^\circ$, a needle, two plates and the seven BLDConograph cells. The two rankings of the 24 sorts agree closely, with Spearman's rank correlation $0.77$ to $0.97$. The two scales are not proportional: SELLA's distance divided by the cell's mean squared edge is a median $0.71$ times the strain distance, with a wide spread between cells. With SELLA's band set by that median ratio, which is calibrated on the same 14 cells and so flatters the agreement, the Bravais types within the band agree in 11 of the 14 cells. They differ on the needle and the two plates, where an absolute band weighs the long edges differently: SELLA admits hP, hR, oF, tI and tP for the needle, which are far in strain, and the strain band admits oF (and tI) for the plates, which SELLA's does not. For these thin cells $r_1$ is $4$ to $6\%$, so their strain distances above it are bounds and are flagged.
